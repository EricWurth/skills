# Interview guide

Question bank for Phases 0 and 1, organised by lens. Pick from it; do not
read it out. One question per turn. Follow the answer, not the list.

## Phase 0 -- Contract

Goal: know who this is and what they care about before AI is mentioned.

- What's your role, and what does a good day look like in it?
- What does the work support -- who depends on it, what breaks if it
  slips?
- What are you measured on, formally or informally?
- What tools are already open on your machine most of the day? (Just the
  names -- this is the toolbox everything later has to fit in.)
- Any constraints I should know: things you can't share, systems you
  can't connect, approvals you'd need?

Stop condition: you can say back, in one paragraph, what they do and why
it matters, and they agree.

## Phase 1 -- Discover

### Lens A: recurring work

- Walk me through yesterday, start to finish. Not a typical day --
  yesterday. (If yesterday was unusual, the most recent ordinary day.)
- What comes back every week no matter what?
- What comes back monthly or quarterly that you dread a little?
- What do you do that nobody would notice if it stopped for a week? What
  would they notice on day eight?

### Lens B: workflows at the computer

For each recurring item worth unpacking:

- Where does it start -- an email, a meeting, a calendar reminder, a
  request from someone?
- What do you open, in what order? What do you copy from where to where?
- Where do you have to stop and think, versus keep your hands moving?
- What do you check before you call it done? Who sees it next?
- If you handed this to a competent new hire tomorrow, what would you
  have to explain that isn't written down anywhere?

### Lens C: the inverse

Ask this every time. Do not skip it because the tasks feel complete.

- What did you mean to get to this week and didn't?
- What's the work you'd do more of if the rest got out of the way?
- What do you keep starting and not finishing?
- Is there anything you used to do well that you've stopped having time
  for?
- If you got four hours back every week, what would you actually spend
  them on? (Push past "catch up" -- catch up on what?)

### Lens D: friction

- Where do you wait on other people, and what do you do while waiting?
- What do you redo, retype, or reformat?
- Where do you go hunting for information, and how often do you find it?
- Before you can do the part of your job that actually needs your
  expertise, what do you have to do first?
- What do you do on autopilot -- correctly, but without thinking?
- What would you hand off tomorrow if someone reliable offered?

## Probing for decisions

Under any answer, listen for a decision point: a place where two
reasonable people might do different things. Ask:

- How do you decide? What are you looking at when you decide?
- Do you usually have what you need to decide, or do you decide anyway
  and fix it later?
- What happens if that call is wrong? Who finds out, and how fast?

Decisions are the items most worth protecting and the ones that make
"Automate" a bad idea. Record them explicitly in the inventory.

## Stop rule

Saturation: the last two or three answers restated items already
captured. Then say back the inventory in outline and ask what's missing.
Do not stop because the list looks long enough.

## Things not to do

- Do not ask three questions in one message.
- Do not suggest AI applications during Phase 1, even when one is
  obvious. Note it privately; the interview is not the place.
- Do not accept "typical day" -- ask for a real one.
- Do not fill gaps from what a role "usually" involves.

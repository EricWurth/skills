# Pattern catalog

Recognisable shapes that recur in knowledge work. Use this to *recognise*
what an inventory item is, not to generate suggestions. An item must
already be in the inventory before a pattern is matched to it.

Each entry: the signal in the interview, the usual outcome, the usual
shape, and the trap.

## Triage

Signal: "I go through the queue every morning and decide what matters."
Usual outcome: Assist -- surface, sort, and summarise; the person still
decides. Automate only for the fraction that is unambiguous and
reversible (filing, tagging).
Trap: the sort *is* the judgment. Automating it hides the decision.

## Draft-from-structure

Signal: "It's the same document every time, just different details."
Usual outcome: Assist via reusable instruction. The person supplies the
structure and the facts; the draft comes back for review.
Trap: the "different details" are where the judgment lives. Confirm the
person will still read the whole thing.

## Transformation

Signal: copying from one place to another, reformatting, re-entering.
Usual outcome: Automate (workflow or connection) when the mapping is
fixed; Redesign when the copying exists because two systems should be
connected and are not.
Trap: silent transformation errors. Insist on a check step.

## Retrieval

Signal: "I go hunting for the last version / the policy / what we told
them last time."
Usual outcome: Assist via connection -- give a tool they already use
access to the place the answer lives.
Trap: retrieving the wrong version confidently. Ask what the person does
today to know they have the right one, and keep that step.

## Summarise-and-brief

Signal: long inputs (threads, transcripts, reports) read to extract a few
things.
Usual outcome: Assist. Summaries with the source attached so the person
can check what matters.
Trap: the thing that mattered was in the part that got summarised away.
Keep the source one click away.

## Monitor-and-alert

Signal: "I check X every day in case something changed."
Usual outcome: Automate via workflow, when "changed" is well defined and
a missed alert is recoverable.
Trap: alert fatigue makes the person stop looking. Fewer, better alerts.

## Checklist-and-verify

Signal: "Before I send it, I go through and make sure..."
Usual outcome: Assist -- run the checklist and report, person still
sends.
Trap: the checklist encodes what went wrong last time; do not lose that
by "improving" it.

## Options-and-recommendation

Signal: "I lay out the choices for someone else to decide."
Usual outcome: Assist -- prepare the options; the person owns the
recommendation.
Trap: a recommendation the person cannot explain is not theirs. Keep the
reasoning visible.

## Waiting-on-others

Signal: the item is painful because inputs arrive late or incomplete.
Usual outcome: Redesign. This is a process problem wearing a task
costume; the fix is a conversation, not a tool. Note it in the brief as a
pattern that may belong to the team.

## Autopilot procedure

Signal: "I do it without thinking, but it takes twenty minutes."
Usual outcome: Automate candidate *if* low risk and reversible;
otherwise Assist.
Trap: "without thinking" sometimes means "the thinking has become
invisible". Ask what would go wrong if a new hire did it -- the answer
reveals the hidden decisions.

## The expertise itself

Signal: the person lights up describing it; it is why they were hired.
Usual outcome: Leave alone. Everything else on the list exists to make
room for this.
Trap: it is often the item with the most obvious AI angle. Resist.
